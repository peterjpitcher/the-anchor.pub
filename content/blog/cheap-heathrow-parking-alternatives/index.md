## Why “cheap Heathrow parking” is harder than it sounds

With changing rates, advance deals and extras, it is hard to tell which option is really the cheapest. Three things are worth checking:

1. **Changing prices** - parking prices can change by date, product and availability.
2. **Extras** - read the small print for damage waivers, late return fees or card charges.
3. **Time** - a wait for a shuttle or a drop-off queue adds to your journey.

## Check live prices for your dates

We haven't typed anyone's prices into this guide, ours included, because they change. Check Heathrow's own site for its car parks, and [our Heathrow parking page](/heathrow-parking) for ours. Then compare the two for the days you're away.

## Option 1: Official Heathrow parking

**Best for:** travellers who want to park at the airport itself.

Heathrow runs its own car parks. Prices, transfers and terms differ by car park and by date, so check Heathrow's own site for your trip.

## Option 2: Meet-and-greet operators

**Best for:** passengers prioritising terminal forecourt drop-offs.

Meet-and-greet firms offer tempting promo codes, but the small print matters. Vehicles are usually stored in off-site compound lots that may be unsurfaced or shared between operators. Watch out for:

- Mandatory damage waivers or extra insurance at check-in
- Charges if your return flight is delayed
- Key-handling risks, your car will be moved multiple times

If you genuinely need a kerbside drop-off, book a reputable provider and read reviews carefully.

## Option 3: Park and fly hotel deals

**Best for:** families starting a holiday with an overnight stay.

Some hotels around Heathrow sell a room and parking together. That can suit you if you want a relaxed evening before an early flight. Check with each hotel for its price, how long you can leave the car and how its transfer works.
