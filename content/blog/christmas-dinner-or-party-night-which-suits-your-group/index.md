Most people searching for a Christmas do near Heathrow are choosing between two completely different products, and nobody ever says so out loud. One is a party night: a shared room in a hotel, a fixed price per head, a set menu and entertainment laid on. The other is a Christmas dinner: your group, your own table, a proper meal, and then you go home. Both are good. Booking the wrong one is how you end up with eleven colleagues sitting in silence next to a dance floor they never asked for.

Here is the honest comparison, including the bit where we tell you we are not the right answer.

## The short answer

If your group wants a dance floor, a DJ and a room full of a few hundred strangers all doing the same thing at the same time, book a hotel party night. That is a genuinely good product and we do not offer it. We stopped running shared Christmas party nights, and we have not replaced them with a watered-down version.

If your group wants to sit down together, eat a Christmas dinner, talk without shouting, and leave when they feel like it, that is what we do. Our festive menu is a real sit-down set menu, not a party night with a different name.

## The two formats, side by side

| What actually differs | Hotel party night | Christmas dinner at a village pub |
|---|---|---|
| Who shares the room | Other groups you have never met, often several hundred people | Your group at your own table, in a pub with regulars in the bar |
| Cost structure | One fixed price per head, drinks usually extra | Each person picks 1, 2 or 3 courses and pays for what they order, plus drinks |
| What is included | A set menu plus entertainment | The meal, the trimmings, and a glass of prosecco for adults, swappable for orange juice |
| Entertainment | The main event | None. No DJ, no live act, no party night. That is deliberate |
| Noise | Loud by design. Hard to talk once the music starts | Pub busy. No dance floor, so a table of eight can actually hear each other |
| Group size that works | Small groups joining a shared room, or large groups filling tables | Minimum 6. Up to 20 as a table booking, above that it is private hire |
| Travel and parking | Check parking charges and how people get home before you commit | 20 free spaces on site, no fees, no time limit |
| Organising effort | Low. Pick a date, pay per head, turn up | Book, pay the deposit, and pre-order if anyone wants 2 or 3 courses |

## Who a hotel party night genuinely suits

You have a mixed team who barely see each other and you want the night to carry itself. Entertainment does a lot of social work: it gives people something to look at when the conversation runs dry, and nobody has to make small talk for three straight hours.

You want the whole thing bundled. One price, one decision. If you are organising for forty people who cannot agree on anything, a fixed package is a mercy.

You want to dance. This is the honest one. A pub dinner has no dance floor at Christmas. If dancing is the point, we are the wrong booking and you will be disappointed.

## Who a sit-down Christmas dinner genuinely suits

You want to hear each other. Teams that genuinely like each other tend to want the conversation, not the DJ, and families almost always do. If the point is catching up, a room built for volume works against you.

You have a group of mixed ages. Children, grandparents and everyone in between do better at a table than in a ballroom. Children are welcome here, and so are dogs, throughout the pub on a lead, with water bowls and biscuits on the house.

You do not want a per-head price you cannot control. Some people want three courses. Some want one and an early night. Each person picks their own tier rather than the whole table committing to the same thing.

You are flying in or driving. Terminal 5 is about 7 minutes away, Terminals 2 and 3 about 11, Terminal 4 about 12, and Junction 14 of the M25 is 2 minutes. We are outside the ULEZ zone, and the 441, 442 and 555 buses stop nearby. Directions are on our [find us page](/find-us).

## What a Christmas dinner at a village pub is honestly like

It is a pub. There will be people at the bar who have not come for Christmas dinner and are just having a pint, because that is what a village pub is for. It is not a private ballroom and it does not pretend to be.

You get your own table. The food comes to you, the trimmings come with it (pigs in blankets, stuffing and brussels sprouts), and adults get a glass of prosecco on every tier, or orange juice instead. Children on the 1-course option get a Fruit Shoot or a small soft drink.

The room is warm and busy rather than hushed, and the noise is people talking rather than amplified music. If that sounds underwhelming, that is a useful signal: you probably want the party night. Plenty of groups read it and think, yes, that is exactly it.

## FAQs

### Does The Anchor do Christmas party nights?

No. We do not run shared Christmas party nights any more. What we do is a sit-down festive set menu for your own group, with 1, 2 or 3 courses chosen per person. If you want a shared party night with entertainment, a hotel package is the right call, and we would rather you booked the thing you actually want.

### What is the smallest group you take for Christmas dinner?

Six guests, and we need at least 24 hours' notice, so no same-day bookings.

### Do we all have to have the same number of courses?

No, and that is one of the real differences from a fixed package. Each person picks their own tier: 1, 2 or 3 courses. The 2 and 3 course tiers need a pre-order, with the deadline confirmed when you book.

### Is there a deposit?

Yes. £10 per person on every Christmas booking whatever the group size, taken at booking. It is non-refundable and it is deducted from your final bill.

### What if we are more than 20 people?

Terminal 5 is about 7 minutes by car (3.8 miles), Terminals 2 and 3 about 11 minutes, Terminal 4 about 12. Parking is free: 20 spaces on site, level, floodlit, with CCTV and no time limit while you are visiting.
