If you are feeding a big group this Christmas, the decision that actually matters is not which food you pick. It is whether people sit down or stand up. Get that right and the rest falls into place. Get it wrong and you spend the evening watching forty people you invited fail to talk to each other.

So this guide is about format, not menus. We run [festive buffets and sit-down Christmas bookings](/christmas-parties) at The Anchor in Stanwell Moor, minutes from the Heathrow terminals, and the question we field most in November is some version of "which one do we need?". Here is how we work it out.

## The short answer

A buffet is a mingling format. A sit-down meal is a conversation format. Almost every other difference follows from that one.

If the point is that people who rarely see each other get to move around and speak to more than five colleagues, you want a buffet. If the point is a proper shared meal with a beginning, a middle and an end, you want the sit-down. Neither is more festive than the other. They are built for different nights.

## What actually changes on the night

| | Festive buffet | Sit-down Christmas menu |
|---|---|---|
| How people move | Constantly, in small shifting groups | Fixed, you talk to your neighbours all night |
| Room capacity | Much higher, because nobody needs a chair | Limited by covers |
| Timing | Loose, people eat when they want | Structured around courses |
| Late arrivals | Absorbed easily | Awkward, they miss a course |
| Ordering admin | One decision for the whole group | Per person, and pre-order for 2 and 3 courses |
| Group minimum | 30 guests, no exceptions | 6 guests |
| Budgeting | One package for the group | Varies with how many courses each person takes |

The row people underestimate is timing. A buffet absorbs guests arriving across an hour without anything going wrong, which matters if half your group is coming off shift, off a flight, or out of an office that never finishes when it says it will. A sit-down meal punishes that. Someone always turns up as the plates go down.

The row people overestimate is food quality. A buffet is not a lesser meal, it is a different delivery mechanism.

## The standing-versus-seated maths

Do this sum before you fall in love with a format. At Christmas we can hold **60 seated or 200 standing**. That is more than triple. Chairs and tables eat floor space at an extraordinary rate.

So if your group is 45, both formats are open and it is a genuine choice about atmosphere. If your group is 80, the decision has been made for you: standing is the only way everyone gets in the same room. And if your group is 20, you are better off sitting down, because 20 people spread around a room built for 200 feels thin rather than lively.

In practice, no buffet is purely standing. People perch, park themselves at the end of a table, drift outside. Our dining room seats 26 and holds 50 standing, with French doors onto the garden, so most large groups end up with a mix: a food area plus seating clusters. Ask for that when you enquire about [private hire](/private-hire) rather than assuming it is one or the other.

## The bit that catches large groups out: getting them all there

Format is the decision. Arrival is the thing that actually goes wrong, and it gets harder the bigger your group is. Forty people means forty separate journeys, and a buffet only absorbs ragged arrivals if people can actually get to you.

## How to actually decide

Three questions, in order.

1. **How many people?** Over 60, buffet. Under 30, sit-down. Between the two, keep reading.
2. **Do you want them moving around?** Mixing across teams or across a family, buffet. A proper meal together, sit-down.
3. **How reliable are the arrival times?** Ragged arrivals point to a buffet every time.

If two of the three agree, that is your format. Send us numbers, a rough date and a rough arrival time, and we will tell you honestly whether the room does what you want. Company bookings can start from our [corporate events page](/corporate-events), or go straight to [booking a table](/book-table) for groups of 20 or fewer.
