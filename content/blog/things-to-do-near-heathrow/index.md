Whether you've got a long layover, a free afternoon before a flight, or you're staying at one of the nearby hotels and need to escape the terminal for a few hours, the area around Heathrow Airport is surprisingly full of things to do. Most people assume the surroundings are all industrial estates and dual carriageways, and fair enough, parts of it are. But within 30 minutes of the airport, you've got royal palaces, world-class gardens, theme parks, and some genuinely brilliant pubs and walks.

Here are 12 things worth doing near Heathrow, with practical details on distance and how long you'll need.

---

## 1. Windsor Castle

**Distance from Heathrow:** 20 minutes by car | **Time needed:** 2-3 hours | **Cost:** See the castle's own site

Windsor Castle is right on Heathrow's doorstep, and it's been a royal residence for over 900 years. The State Apartments don't feel like a roped-off museum: the rooms are still used for state occasions.

The town of Windsor itself is worth an hour of wandering too, with cobbled streets, independent shops, and pubs and restaurants along the river. Check the castle's own site for opening days, ticket prices and the Changing of the Guard before you go.

---

## 2. Plane Spotting from a Beer Garden

**Distance from Heathrow:** 7 minutes from T5 | **Time needed:** 1-2 hours | **Cost:** Free (plus whatever you eat and drink)

The village of Stanwell Moor sits directly under Heathrow's southern runway approach path. At busy times a plane comes over about every 90 seconds, 500 to 800 feet up. A380s, Boeing 777s, 787 Dreamliners, the lot. [The Anchor's beer garden](/plane-spotting-heathrow) is a good spot to watch them, with 64 seats outdoors and full food and drink service during kitchen hours.

Kids absolutely love it. Adults pretend they're only watching because the kids are, but nobody's fooling anyone. There's free WiFi throughout, so you can pull up Flightradar24 and identify every aircraft as it comes over.

Planes operate on a rotating weekly schedule, one week landings are overhead until 3pm, the next week from 3pm. So check which week you're in for the best experience.

**Top tip:** Download Flightradar24 before you go. Half the fun is knowing whether that's an A380 or a 777 before it comes over.

## 3. Kew Gardens

**Distance from Heathrow:** 25 minutes by car | **Time needed:** 3-4 hours | **Cost:** See Kew's own site

The Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. That sounds dry, but the reality is anything but. The Temperate House alone is worth the trip.

The Treetop Walkway takes you up through the canopy. Spring and early summer are the best times, but even in winter the glasshouses are worth seeing.

Check Kew's own site for opening times, ticket prices, parking and how to get there by public transport.

## 4. A Pre-Flight Pub Lunch

**Distance from Heathrow:** 7 minutes from T5 | **Time needed:** 1-2 hours | **Cost:** See the [food menu](/food-menu)

If your flight isn't until the evening, there's no reason to eat at the terminal. A proper pub lunch a few minutes from the airport is far more relaxing.

[The Anchor in Stanwell Moor](/restaurants-near-heathrow) is the closest traditional pub to Heathrow, seven minutes from Terminal 5, and does proper British pub food. Fish and chips, pies, burgers and stone-baked pizzas, all priced live on the [food menu](/food-menu). They've got [20 free parking spaces](/near-heathrow) and luggage storage. The pub isn't open in the morning, so check [the opening and kitchen hours](/find-us) before you plan around it.

If you are working to a departure time, [book a table](/book-table) before you set off or call 01753 682707, and the table is waiting when you arrive.

## 5. Thorpe Park

**Distance from Heathrow:** 25 minutes by car | **Time needed:** Full day | **Cost:** See Thorpe Park's own site

If you want proper thrills, Thorpe Park is a theme park less than half an hour from the airport, with big coasters including Nemesis Inferno, Stealth and The Swarm.

Check Thorpe Park's own site for opening dates, ticket prices and parking before you go.

## 6. Richmond Park

**Distance from Heathrow:** 30 minutes by car | **Time needed:** 2-3 hours | **Cost:** Free

London's largest Royal Park is home to over 600 red and fallow deer, and it feels like actual countryside despite being in Zone 4. The park covers 2,500 acres, big enough that you can walk for an hour without retracing your steps.

The view from King Henry's Mound towards St Paul's Cathedral is protected by law and stretches 12 miles across London. On a clear day, it's one of the best views in the city. Cycling is popular here too.

Isabella Plantation, a woodland garden within the park, is at its best in late April and May when the azaleas are in bloom.

## 7. Legoland Windsor

**Distance from Heathrow:** 25 minutes by car | **Time needed:** Full day | **Cost:** See Legoland's own site

If you're travelling with kids aged 3-12, Legoland is built for them. The Miniland area, where they've built London, Amsterdam, and other cities entirely from Lego, is worth seeing whatever your age.

Check Legoland's own site for opening dates, ticket prices and parking before you go.

## 8. A Beer Garden Under the Flight Path

**Distance from Heathrow:** 7 minutes from T5 | **Time needed:** As long as you like | **Cost:** A pint and good company

This is different from dedicated plane spotting, it's more about the atmosphere. Sitting in a [proper pub beer garden](/beer-garden) with a cold drink while wide-body aircraft descend overhead at regular intervals is a uniquely Heathrow-area experience. It sounds noisy on paper, but the reality is more impressive than annoying. The planes are so close you can read the livery.

The Anchor's garden in Stanwell Moor has 64 seats, and dogs are welcome on a lead. There's full food and drink service during kitchen hours. It's outside the ULEZ zone.

On a warm summer evening, a drink in the garden watching aircraft come in overhead is one of the better things to do near Heathrow. One week planes land overhead until 3pm, the next from 3pm onwards, so check which week you're in.

The garden fills up quickly once the sun is out, so [book a garden table](/book-table) if you want your spot held. Walk-ins are welcome too, and dogs are welcome throughout.

## 9. Thames Path Walking

**Distance from Heathrow:** 15-20 minutes to Staines or Windsor | **Time needed:** 1-4 hours | **Cost:** Free

The Thames Path runs right through the Heathrow area, and the stretches between Staines, Runnymede, and Windsor are some of the prettiest in the whole 184-mile trail. Flat, well-maintained, and with plenty of pub stops along the way.

The section from Staines to Windsor (about 6 miles) takes you past the site where Magna Carta was sealed in 1215 at Runnymede, there are memorials there that are worth a stop. The walk is easy going, entirely flat, and suitable for all fitness levels.

For a shorter walk, the loop around the reservoirs near Stanwell Moor is popular with birdwatchers. You'll see herons, cormorants, and various wading birds, with planes coming over for added entertainment.

**Starting points:** Staines riverside car park, Runnymede meadows, or Windsor promenade.

## 10. Shopping at Westfield or Staines High Street

**Distance from Heathrow:** 20-30 minutes | **Time needed:** 2-3 hours | **Cost:** Depends on your willpower

If retail therapy is your thing, you've got options. Westfield London in Shepherd's Bush is a large shopping centre towards central London.

Closer to the airport, Staines town centre has the Two Rivers shopping centre and a high street.

For something different, Windsor Royal Station has shops and cafes inside the old railway station, right next to the castle.

## 11. A Spa Day

**Distance from Heathrow:** 5-30 minutes | **Time needed:** Half or full day | **Cost:** Check with the hotel or spa

Some Heathrow hotels sell spa day packages that you don't need to be a guest to use. What's on offer, the price and the availability all vary, so check with the hotel.

A spa morning followed by a late flight is a seriously civilised way to end a trip.

**Top tip:** Book in advance.

---

## 12. Explore Stanwell Moor Village

**Distance from Heathrow:** 7 minutes from T5 | **Time needed:** 1-2 hours | **Cost:** Free (plus refreshments)

This is the local option. Stanwell Moor is a small village next to the airport that's managed to keep its identity despite having Heathrow as a neighbour. The reservoir walks are peaceful, and the community feel is real.

It's the kind of place where you go for a quiet pint at the local pub and end up chatting to someone who's lived there for 40 years. Not every day out needs to involve a queue, a gift shop, and a car park the size of a football pitch.

The village is outside the ULEZ zone, there's free parking at the pub, and the walks around the nearby reservoirs are genuinely pleasant, especially in the mornings when the birdlife is most active.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is there to do near Heathrow Airport for free?

Plane spotting, Richmond Park, Thames Path walks, and exploring local villages like Stanwell Moor are all free. Several of the best experiences near Heathrow don't cost a penny.

### Is there anything to do near Heathrow during a layover?

Yes. With a few hours between flights you can get to a local pub for lunch and back, and a longer layover opens up Windsor or Kew Gardens. Check that you're allowed to enter the UK, and how early your airline wants you back, before you plan to leave the airport.

### What can families do near Heathrow?

Legoland and Thorpe Park are the obvious choices for a full day. For something shorter, plane spotting with kids at a beer garden near the flight path is free and endlessly entertaining for young aviation fans. At The Anchor's [beer garden](/beer-garden), children are welcome at all hours. High chairs, buggy space and bottle warming on request are all here, and breastfeeding is welcome. We don't have baby changing facilities. You can [book a table](/book-table) if you would rather not chance it on a sunny weekend.

### Where can I eat near Heathrow that isn't airport food?

There are pubs and restaurants within a few minutes of the terminals. [The Anchor in Stanwell Moor](/restaurants-near-heathrow) is the closest traditional pub (7 mins from T5), with free parking and prices live on the [food menu](/food-menu). You can [book a table](/book-table) or call 01753 682707. See our guide to [restaurants near Heathrow](/restaurants-near-heathrow) for more options.

### Is there parking near these attractions?
