Someone has to organise the work Christmas do, and this year it is you. The first decision is not the venue. It is what kind of night you are actually buying, because the budget, the shortlist and the arguments all follow from that one choice.

We take Christmas bookings at The Anchor in Stanwell Moor, seven minutes from Terminal 5. This guide is written from your side of the table, including the part where we tell you when we are the wrong answer.

## First, work out which night your team actually wants

Search near the airport and a lot of what comes back is a hotel party night: a shared ballroom, a ticket price per head, entertainment laid on, and other companies in the room with you. That is a real format and plenty of teams love it.

It is not what we do. We stopped running shared party nights, and we do not put on a DJ, a disco or a Christmas entertainment programme. We give you your own table in a village pub with a festive set menu, then leave you to your evening. If you are still weighing those two products against each other, we have compared them properly in [Christmas dinner or party night](/blog/christmas-dinner-or-party-night-which-suits-your-group).

So the sorting question is this:

| If your team wants... | Book... |
|---|---|
| A dance floor, a DJ and a big night out | An airport hotel party night, not us |
| Their own table, a decent meal and a conversation you can hear | A pub |
| Some of both | Split the night: eat somewhere, move on afterwards |

Take that third row seriously. A mixed group almost always holds people who want a big one and people who want a good dinner and to be home by half ten. Feed everyone properly first, then let the late-night contingent carry on elsewhere. Nobody is offended, and you have not spent the budget on a ticket half the team did not want.

If the pub option fits, the format and dates are on our [Christmas party page](/christmas-parties).

## The group sizes that change what you are booking

The number in your spreadsheet quietly changes which product you are buying.

- **Fewer than 6 guests.** Not a Christmas booking. Our festive menu needs a minimum of 6.
- **6 to 20 guests.** A normal table booking. You book it, you turn up, we look after you.
- **More than 20 guests.** No longer a table booking. This is [private hire](/private-hire), and it goes through a person rather than a form: manager@the-anchor.pub, 01753 682707, or WhatsApp on the same number.

Our Christmas capacity is 60 seated and 200 standing, so a large team is doable. It just needs a conversation, because at that size you are agreeing space and layout, not a table.

Every Christmas booking needs at least 24 hours' notice, and the service window runs from 10 November to 20 December 2026. The popular slots go early, so book far sooner than that.

## Sit-down meal or buffet: the number decides it for you

Organisers agonise over this. Usually the minimum makes the decision for them.

**Festive buffets need 30 guests, minimum, no exceptions.** There are three packages: Festive Sandwich & Salad, Festive Hot Finger, and Festive Premium Grazing. If your team is 18 people, the buffet is not on the table and you can stop weighing it up.

If you are over 30 and could go either way, the question is how the night behaves. A sit-down meal fixes people in seats: the same six conversations all evening, but a clear beginning, middle and end. A buffet keeps people moving, which suits several teams who barely know each other, though it removes the shape of the evening. There is no speech moment in a buffet. The full version of that argument, including the capacity maths, is in [buffet or sit-down for large groups](/blog/festive-buffet-ideas-for-large-groups-near-heathrow).

## Per-person course choice kills the usual menu argument

The most tedious part of organising a work do is normally the fight over which set menu the whole table commits to. One person wants three courses. The person driving home to relieve a babysitter wants one, and a bill they are not embarrassed by.

On our festive menu that is not a table decision. Each guest picks 1, 2 or 3 courses individually, so nobody subsidises anybody else's pudding and you are not refereeing.

The practical detail:

- **1 course** needs a pre-book but **no pre-order**. Handy for the colleague who confirms at the last minute.
- **2 and 3 courses** need a pre-order. That deadline is confirmed with your booking.
- There is **no kids 2 or 3 course tier**, and no child price at those tiers. Children can order the adult 2 or 3 course at the adult price.
- **Adults get a glass of prosecco** on all three tiers, swappable for orange juice. **Children get a Fruit Shoot or a small soft drink** with the 1 course.
- **Trimmings included:** pigs in blankets, stuffing and brussels sprouts.

The menu is released closer to the time, and prices come from our live system rather than a leaflet, so ask us for current ones when you enquire. For allergen queries, see menu or contact us for allergen information.

## The deposit, and why per-person deposits help you

Every Christmas booking carries a £10 per person deposit, whatever the size of the party. Taken at booking, non-refundable, and deducted from your bill on the night.

Organisers flinch at that line. They should not, because the deposit is doing a job for you. Work Christmas headcounts drift: twenty-two say yes in October, nineteen turn up in December, and you are the one explaining the gap. A per-person deposit turns a casual "yeah, put me down" into a small commitment, so your final number is closer to the truth. It also gives you a clean reason to collect from colleagues while the do is still a nice idea, rather than chasing them in January.

Because it comes off the bill, it is not an extra cost. Same money, paid sooner.

## What usually goes wrong

- **Booking the format before asking the team.** Send two lines: big night out, or good dinner? You get an answer and cover for the decision.
- **Assuming a buffet is available.** Under 30 people, it is not.
- **Treating a group of 25 as a table booking.** Over 20 is private hire, and the earlier that starts, the more choice you have.
- **Leaving the pre-order to the last week.** Chasing 18 people for a course choice in mid-December is nobody's idea of festive.
- **One person fronting the whole bill.** Sort the money before the night, not while wearing a paper hat.

At least 6. Up to 20 is a standard table booking. Above 20 becomes private hire, arranged with us directly on 01753 682707 or manager@the-anchor.pub.

No. We stopped running shared party nights, and we do not put on a DJ or a Christmas entertainment programme. You get your own table and a festive set menu in a village pub. If your team wants a dance floor, an airport hotel party night will suit them better than we will.

Every Christmas booking takes a £10 per person deposit at booking, regardless of party size. It is non-refundable and deducted from your final bill on the night.

No. Each guest chooses 1, 2 or 3 courses individually. The 2 and 3 course options need a pre-order, with the deadline confirmed when you book; 1 course needs no pre-order. There is no kids 2 or 3 course tier, so children ordering those tiers pay the adult price.
