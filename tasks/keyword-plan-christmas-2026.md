# Keyword Plan: Christmas 2026 (target country: United Kingdom)

Built 29 July 2026. Two GKP rounds, UK / English.

**Sources and dates**
- Google Keyword Planner, exports 29 Jul 2026, data window 1 Jul 2025 to 30 Jun 2026 (includes a full Christmas peak)
- Google Search Console, export 28 Jul 2026, window 26 Apr to 26 Jul 2026, query filter `christmas`
- Landscape research via US-locale WebSearch on 29 Jul 2026, market included in queries. Directional only, **not** a verified UK SERP observation

**Confidence: medium.** GKP volumes are bucketed ranges (this account has no ad spend), so every figure is a
bucket floor, not a point value. All 12 monthly seasonality columns exported blank in both rounds, so the
November and December curve is **not measured**. Seasonality below is inferred from the three-month change column.

---

## Headline findings

### 1. The Heathrow keyword set has almost no measurable demand

38 of 56 Round 1 terms returned no data, and **34 of those 38 carried a local modifier**. Dataless terms included
`christmas party venue heathrow`, `christmas party pub heathrow`, `office christmas party heathrow`,
`corporate christmas party heathrow`, `work christmas party heathrow`, `christmas venue hire heathrow`.

Every local term that *did* report came back at the 50 floor bucket. GKP blank means unknown rather than zero,
but a 34-of-38 pattern plus floor-bucket reporting on the survivors is a consistent signal, not noise.

**Implication: do not build paid or organic around town-modifier keywords.** Use radius targeting against
"near me" and generic terms instead. That is how local intent actually reaches a physical venue.

### 2. GKP independently confirms the July GSC spike was not real demand

`christmas party heathrow` averages **50 searches a month** across a window that already contains a Christmas peak.
GSC recorded 654 impressions for `christmas party in heathrow`, nearly all on 24 July 2026.

Fifty searches a month cannot produce that. Two independent sources now agree the spike was a Google
re-evaluation burst following the 19 and 21 July consolidation, not customer demand. Do not plan against it.

### 3. The near-me keyword set is roughly four times more efficient

GKP forecast, both at £20/day, August 2026, UK, Maximize Conversions:

| Metric | Round 1 set | Round 2 set |
|---|---|---|
| Estimated clicks | 127.1 | 435.71 |
| Estimated impressions | 3,065.56 | 10,718.21 |
| Estimated avg CPC | £4.88 | £1.42 |
| Conversion rate | 3.6% | 4.85% |
| Conversions | 5 | 21 |
| Average CPA | £140.00 | £29.00 |

Caveat: this is GKP's own model on an account with no history, and August is out of season. Treat the
*ratio* as the finding, not the absolute numbers.

### 4. LOCALISED AND SEASONAL FORECAST (supersedes the UK-wide numbers above)

Run 29 Jul 2026 in Google Ads account 395-587-0517, Forecast tab, after correcting two faults: the
Forecast tab carried its **own** location setting still fixed to United Kingdom (independent of the
Historical metrics tab), and Google's autocomplete had inserted **Uxbridge, Massachusetts** and
**Sloughhouse, California** because arrow-key selection picks the second dropdown row, not the first.

Locations set (13): Egham, Feltham, Hounslow, Maidenhead, Richmond, Shepperton, Slough,
Staines-upon-Thames, Stanwell, Sunbury-on-Thames, Twickenham, Walton-on-Thames, Windsor.

| Scenario, all localised, Maximize conversions | Conversions | CPA | CPC | Clicks | Impressions | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 2026, £20/day | 5 | £120 | £6.03 | 103 | 2K | £620 |
| **1 Nov to 20 Dec 2026, £20/day** | **32** | **£31** | **£1.76** | **569** | **12K** | **£1,000** |
| 1 Nov to 20 Dec 2026, £40/day | 45 | £44 | £2.39 | 837 | 17K | £2,000 |

**Seasonality dominates localisation.** An August localised forecast looks catastrophic at £120 CPA
purely because August is dead for Christmas terms. In the real selling window the CPA returns to £31,
which is where the UK-wide run had it all along.

**£20/day is the efficient point.** Doubling to £40/day buys 41% more conversions for 42% worse CPA,
and GKP's own budget curve flattens above roughly £60/day.

**Revised budget: about £1,000 across 1 Nov to 20 Dec at £20/day, plus a smaller September and October
presence to build conversion data. Roughly £1,500 total, NOT the £3,225 originally proposed.**

**Watch Richmond.** In the £40/day per-location breakdown Richmond took 414 of 837 clicks and £985 of
£2,000. It is the furthest town in the set and will dominate spend if left uncapped.

Expected contribution: 32 enquiries at a 40% close is roughly 13 bookings, about 40% fill of the 12
peak Friday and Saturday sessions. Paid search alone does **not** reach the 30 bookings needed for 55%
fill, so Meta and direct B2B outreach remain necessary, not optional.

## Avoid / negative keywords

- **shared christmas party** (500, Medium 37) and **festive party night** (50): the trade meaning is a shared
  party night with disco. Per `docs/SSOT.md`, shared party nights are **discontinued**. Targeting these buys a
  mismatch. Negative them.
- **buffet food ideas** (5,000 but **High, index 100**): maximum advertiser contest, purely informational.
  Organic only, never paid.
- **office party food ideas** (50, **High, index 97**) and **christmas party west london** (50, High 86) and
  **christmas party ascot** (50, High 71): high contest, negligible volume. Skip.
- **Christmas Day and Boxing Day terms**: the service window closes 20 December. Negative them.
- Out-of-catchment towns from the GSC export: Waterloo, Clerkenwell, Fitzrovia, Belgravia, Harrow, Wilmslow,
  Stockton Heath, Waterlooville and similar.

### Warning on `christmas party night`

5,000 volume at Low competition looks attractive, but a material share of that intent is shared party nights,
which The Anchor does not sell. Either land it on copy that makes the private-booking model obvious, or drop it.
Do not let it into a paid campaign untested.

## Per-cluster implementation guidance

### Cluster 1: near-me Christmas dining (the priority)
- **Title:** already good. `Christmas Party Venue Near Heathrow & Staines | The Anchor`
- **H1:** current H1 works. `Christmas parties near Heathrow, Staines and Surrey`
- **Intent:** someone nearby wants a Christmas meal out and is letting Google use their location
- **Sections needed:** the page already covers menu tiers, booking rules, sittings. The gap is explicit
  "near me" satisfaction: distance and drive times from surrounding towns, parking, and the ULEZ position
- **Cannibalisation:** none. `/christmas-parties` is the consolidated hub as of 19 July 2026

### Cluster 2: Christmas dinner booking
- Same page. This is the lowest-competition real-volume cluster in the whole dataset (index 22, and several
  variants at index 0 to 4). Make the booking path unmistakable above the fold

### Cluster 3: function room hire near me
- **Page:** `/private-hire`, not the Christmas hub
- **Cannibalisation risk:** real. `/private-hire` and `/christmas-parties` both discuss function space.
  Keep `/private-hire` on year-round hire and the Christmas hub on the seasonal offer, cross-linked

### Cluster 5: Christmas buffet ideas (content)
- **Organic only.** Informational intent, and the highest-volume sibling is at competition index 100
- This answers the GSC question we parked: `buffet food ideas christmas in heathrow` had 974 impressions but
  the demand is national and informational, not local and commercial. Serve it with content, never with ad spend

## Measurement plan

- Impressions 5,630 · Clicks 0 · CTR 0% · Average position 30.3
- `/christmas-parties`: 5,397 impressions, 0 clicks, position 30.04
- Page-1 impressions: 395 of 5,630 (7.0%), all on Staines queries

**Review window:** 4 to 6 weeks after changes, so early to mid September 2026, then again mid November.

**What confirms success:**
- Impressions on the near-me cluster = visibility working
- CTR = the metadata fix and SERP relevance working
- Average position on `christmas dinner booking` and the pub-dining variants = the winnable clusters landing
- Paid CPA against the £29 forecast = whether the model held

**Warning:** ignore raw GSC impression totals for the next month. The 24 July re-evaluation burst inflates
any window containing it. Compare like-for-like periods that exclude 24 and 25 July.

## Rationale

The plan moves the whole activation off town-modifier keywords, which the data shows barely exist, and onto
"near me" and pub-dining clusters that carry real volume at low competition and a quarter of the cost per
conversion. Local relevance is then delivered by radius targeting rather than by keyword text, which is how
Google actually resolves local intent for a physical venue. The informational buffet demand that dominated
the GSC export is real but national and non-commercial, so it is routed to content rather than ad spend.
